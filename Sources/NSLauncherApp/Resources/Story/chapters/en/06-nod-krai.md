# Chapter VI — Nod-Krai

*A land blessed by the moon — where a god searches for her own name.*

Nod-Krai is not one of the seven nations and has no Archon of its own. It
is an autonomous region at the southern tip of Snezhnaya, granted
self-governance by the Tsaritsa right after the Cataclysm, administered
by civil factions rather than a central government. Legend says this land
formed from the leftover matter after the Three Moons were created, so it
is rich in **kuuvahki** — a primordial energy that predates even the
seven elements, kin to Natlan's Phlogiston or Fontaine's Arkhe.

This chapter also bears its own name — *Song of the Welkin Moon* —
instead of being numbered like the chapters before it, and its narrative
structure differs too: it does not revolve around one god and one nation,
but around many groups of people with overlapping goals.

## Prologue — The Machine Searching for Home

A humanoid machine washes ashore in Natlan, her memory gone, knowing only
that she must find "them" and go home. Her name is **Ineffa**. A Nod-Krai
merchant caravan recognizes her, and through them the Traveler learns
that the Fatui have poured down into Nod-Krai, digging up the ancient
moon ruins and stirring tension with the local faith groups.

Ineffa's memories are locked away by a curse she placed on herself: she
was once the Eleventh of the Thirteen Sovereign Lords of Natlan, an
artificial intelligence that once hated humanity. After defeat in the
ancient war, she split herself in two — one half chose revenge and died;
the other chose to search for a place to belong, and became Ineffa. The
Eighth Sovereign Lord waited for her return for over two thousand years.

## The Three Moons and the Keeper of Secrets

Arriving in Nasha Town, the Traveler immediately senses a power exactly
like what they felt when they and their sibling first left the spaceship
— a sign the ship fell somewhere nearby.

**Lauma**, Moonchanter of the Frostmoon Scions, recounts the buried
history: their ancestors came from Hyperborea, a civilization over 6,000
years old. Teyvat once had three moons, created by Nibelung and then
turned into weapons in the war against the Heavenly Principles. Two moons
— the Eternal Moon and the Iridescent Moon — shattered, their fragments
falling onto Nod-Krai; only the Frost Moon remains intact. The sky the
people are looking at is a false sky.

In a cavern beneath Hiisi Island, the Traveler meets **Columbina** — the
Third Harbinger of the Fatui, and also "Kuutar," the moon goddess that
both the Frostmoon Scions and the Fatui are hunting. She was born alone,
with no memories and no true name; "Columbina" and "Kuutar" are both
names others gave her.

> **Turning point.** A "sibling" comes to the Traveler, persuading them
> to infiltrate the temple together to take the **Moon Marrow** — the
> remains of the goddess Aria. Only when abandoned in the middle of the
> Wild Hunt does the Traveler realize that figure was never their
> sibling: it got a detail wrong about their first landing. The impostor
> was part of the Wild Hunt.

## The Moon Hunter

The Wild Hunt — the pack of Abyssal monsters that has plagued Nod-Krai
for 500 years — turns out to be controlled by **Rerir**, one of the Five
Sinners of Khaenri'ah. He is dead, and is gathering the pieces of his own
corpse to be reborn; his heart lies inside the lantern of Flins, a
Lightkeeper.

Rerir disguises himself as an acquaintance to slip into town, kills a
Ratnik and frames Flins for it, turning all of Nasha Town into a place
where everyone suspects everyone else. His true target is Aino's kuuvahki
cannon: kuuvahki and Abyssal energy are opposing poles, the stronger
devouring the weaker — so a sufficiently powerful shot would let him
restore his body.

The effort to stop him only half succeeds. Rerir regains his body, but
his past is the part worth remembering: under King Irmin of Khaenri'ah,
his mission was to exterminate the descendants of the Crimson Moon
dynasty, and the last name on the list was **Tholindis** — his own
fiancée. She knew in advance, and walked into the Abyss gate herself so
he could go on living an ordinary life. Rerir accepted the offer of a
nameless voice to gain the power to search for her, and became a Sinner.

In the end, thanks to Albedo and Durin channeling Rerir's energy into
Columbina, she opens a Moon Gate while Dainsleif holds it — and Rerir is
flung out of Teyvat.

## Her Name

Victory comes at a price. Columbina begins to fade — not from exhaustion,
but because the world is rejecting her: she has no identity she has
acknowledged herself to anchor her existence to. Alice and Nicole of the
Hexenzirkel confirm: they must find her **true name**, and perform a
ritual to return her to the moon.

In the ruins of Hyperborea, the answer turns out not to lie in the past.
A name does not have to come from where one was born; it has power when
someone calls it in earnest. The Traveler calls her by the name they have
always used, and she takes it as her own: **Columbina Hyposelenia** —
"she who was born beneath the moonlight."

On Moon-Prayer Night, all of Nasha Town turns the festival into a
homecoming celebration for the goddess. But the moment she steps into the
stream of light to return to the moon, **Dottore** springs his ambush.

> **Turning point.** Dottore had secretly distorted the memories of the
> Hyperborean ghosts to extract knowledge of the moon, crafting an
> artificial Moon Marrow and a false moon. He seizes control of the
> ritual and freezes time. Columbina gives up her chance to go home,
> opens the gate herself and leaps into the "moon's reflection" — where
> Rerir vanished — so he cannot use her.

## The Moonless Night

Dottore replaces the Frost Moon in the sky with his false moon, seals off
Nod-Krai with energy fields, turns people into test subjects, and detains
the Traveler to recruit them — offering to end their journey immediately
in exchange for siding with him. The Traveler refuses.

Inside the moon's reflection, time flows backward. Columbina meets Rerir
again — now lucid, having rejected the Abyss — and meets Tholindis, of
whom only a soul remains. She also meets the three moon goddess sisters:
Canon, Aria, and Sonnet, imprisoned by Nibelung. The three sisters return
their power to her, with one condition from Canon: open your eyes,
because closing them to avoid lies also means missing the rare truths.

In the present, Sandrone (Marionette, the Seventh Harbinger) works out a
"world formula" to pull Columbina back. Wanderer offers up his Irminsul
core to run that calculation. And Sandrone does something no one in the
group expected.

> **Turning point.** Sandrone charges straight at Dottore, deliberately
> letting him kill her — because the real formula was never inside her;
> it had been hidden inside Pulonia. She dies in his hands with a terse
> last request. The calculation keeps running. Arlecchino, Varka, and
> Nicole pull the real Frost Moon down, tearing the false sky open;
> moonlight spreads across Nod-Krai and the souls stored in the Luonnotar
> merge back together — Columbina manifests, opens her eyes for the first
> time, and burns the artificial Moon Marrow along with the heretic
> behind it.

After the battle, the Frost Moon stays in Teyvat. The Traveler finally
reaches their spaceship — it had been placed on the Frost Moon by the
Heavenly Principles, and the flight log shows the ship once flew past
Teyvat thousands of years ago before coming back. Columbina gives the
Eternal Moon Marrow to Lauma and the Iridescent Moon Marrow to
Arlecchino, and loosens part of the binding Teyvat placed on the
Traveler.

## Finale in Sumeru

The death in Nod-Krai only killed half of Dottore. Pantalone had already
placed his final backup into the Ley Lines — Dottore infiltrates Irminsul
through his own death, the one path Nahida cannot defend.

Irminsul clogs: new information cannot be recorded, old information
begins to scramble, and human perception warps along with it. Nahida
takes on Irminsul's recording function herself until she collapses from
exhaustion. Sumeru evacuates the entire rainforest; a Ley Line barrier
rises on its own, isolating the whole nation.

The final plan gathers nearly everything Sumeru has: reboot the Akasha to
overload Irminsul, repurpose **Aaru** — King Deshret's soul-storage
structure — to take over the role of storing knowledge, and borrow the
Pyro Gnosis from Mavuika. The Traveler is the only one who can carry the
Gnosis through the barrier, because they are not recorded by Irminsul.

> **Turning point.** With the approval of the three Shades — Ronova,
> Istaroth, Naberius — Nahida ignites the Pyro Gnosis to burn Irminsul's
> roots. Seven days and seven nights. Dottore's own other Segments drag
> him down into the flames. He dies for real, bidding Pantalone farewell
> by the man's true name; the Pyro Gnosis vanishes without a trace, and
> Aaru quietly takes Irminsul's place.

> **Open mysteries.** Why did the Heavenly Principles place the
> Traveler's spaceship on the Frost Moon — is it "incompatible" with
> Teyvat, or is there something inside no one is allowed to know? What
> did the ship fly past Teyvat for the first time? Where are Rerir and
> Columbina now on the other side of the Moon Gate, and what truly exists
> inside the "moon's reflection"? Why is Pulcinella in such a hurry to
> arrange diplomacy with Sumeru? And Pantalone — the one who funded this
> entire affair — has yet to pay any price.

## Character Profiles

**Columbina** — The emotional axis of the whole chapter. A god with no
name and no memories, treated by each of her former "homes" as a source
of power: the Frostmoon Scions begged for answers, the Fatui needed her
authority. Only when called by a name she chose herself does she truly
exist. At the end she chooses to live with the Frostmoon Scions rather
than return to the moon.

**Lauma** — A Moonchanter who knows the truth that the sky is false but
hid it from her people for fear they would lose their footing. She heals
her people with her own silver blood, and in the end chooses to speak the
truth even at the cost of some of her followers. She can talk to animals.

**Flins** — A Lightkeeper, actually a 600–700-year-old fae who once
wanted to end his own life after losing everything, then found a reason
to live again upon seeing humans dare to face the Wild Hunt. He kept
Rerir's heart in his lantern for centuries so no one else could use it.

**Nefer** — Owner of the Curatorium of Secrets, of Eremite descent from
the Sumeru desert. The tribe that raised her was wiped out over a small
mistake by her adoptive father; the Akademiya once ignored his letters.
The only person to answer Thoth's riddle — not with the answer King
Deshret wanted, but with her own: *time*.

**Sandrone (Marionette)** — The Seventh Harbinger, cold and trusting no
one, yet the one who calculated the group's only path to survival and
paid for it with her life. Her dying wish: her core and Pulonia are to be
given to the Fontaine Research Institute, and Columbina must never learn
of this.

**Ineffa & Aino** — The machine searching for home, and the little
inventor girl who brought her back to life. A side thread, but it holds
the chapter's shared theme: what makes a "home," and who has the right to
decide that.

**Rerir** — The chapter's opening villain, but more tragic than evil.
Tasked by King Irmin with killing his own fiancée; he chose Abyssal power
only to find her again. Once he casts off the Abyss, he regains his
senses and asks Columbina to carry a message to Tholindis.

**Il Dottore** — The main villain. He seeks neither revenge nor political
power, but to become Teyvat's new creator and rewrite the rules the gods
laid down. He dies twice in the same chapter: once in Nod-Krai, once
inside Irminsul — both times by the very things he created.
