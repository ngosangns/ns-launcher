# Chapter IV — Fontaine

*A queen who played a role for 500 years, and the final trial that judged
herself.*

Fontaine is a society of extreme legalism, where every dispute is settled
in the supreme court by a machine capable of "reading" the truth from
memories. The court is headed by Iudex Neuvillette; the nation is
nominally headed by the Hydro Archon Furina — a young, ostentatious queen
who always keeps her distance from her people.

## The Opening Case

An audience member dies mysteriously in the middle of a magic show.
Furina hastily convicts the magician Lyney; the Traveler is assigned the
role of defense by Neuvillette. The investigation gradually reveals a far
greater conspiracy: ancient stone tablets prophesying a great flood that
will soon drown Fontaine, and the image of a goddess weeping alone amid a
sea of water.

## The Crack Behind the Crown

The next series of trials shows that the dam system holding back
Fontaine's water level is weakening — if it breaks, the prophecy will
come true. Neuvillette unexpectedly intervenes with power far beyond that
of an ordinary judge, leaving the first sign that he is no ordinary man.

> **Turning point.** At the trial that judged her, the truth was laid
> bare: 500 years ago, the true Hydro Archon — Focalors — split herself
> in two, hiding the divine half and giving the human half the form of
> Furina, built to play the role of a perfect queen. An ancient curse
> demanded a sacrifice to be undone, and Focalors spent nearly five
> centuries quietly preparing to turn the very machine of judgment into
> the instrument of her own condemnation.

When the Oratrice prepared to execute Furina, Focalors finally appeared
in truth and revealed: Neuvillette is the Hydro Dragon Sovereign, a
primordial being she had brought to Fontaine in human form to learn how
to empathize with humanity. Focalors accepted letting her own machine end
her existence, returning the Hydro elemental authority to its rightful
owner. The prophesied flood still happened, but because Neuvillette
stepped forward to bear the verdict, no one had to dissolve into an
oceanid as the curse had threatened.

Furina, now only an ordinary human, finally gained the thing she had
craved for 499 years: the right to live as herself.

> **Open mysteries.** What is the true role of the Dragon Sovereigns in
> the big picture between Celestia and Teyvat? How is Egeria's curse
> connected to the Abyss Order or the ruins of Khaenri'ah? What is the
> "Heavenly Principles" behind Celestia, really?

## Sidebar: The Spina di Rosula Legacy

Three years before Chapter IV, Callas — Navia's father and former leader
of the Spina di Rosula knightly society — fell terminally ill but hid it
from his daughter, deliberately arranging a duel so that his close friend
Clorinde would "kill" him, both to protect Navia from another threat and
to spare her from watching him slowly die of disease. That misunderstood
death caused the Spina di Rosula's reputation to collapse and badly
fractured the relationship between Navia and Clorinde, before the two
gradually reconciled. The story does not directly expand the great
mysteries, but it digs deep into Fontaine's core theme: the boundary
between justice by law and emotional truth — in the same spirit as the
trial that judged Furina.

## Character Profiles

**Wriothesley** — Once sentenced by Neuvillette himself and exiled to the
Fortress of Meropide, he later challenged the old warden and won control,
reforming the prison into a more orderly system. When the primordial
seawater threatened to drown Meropide, he was the one who evacuated the
prisoners while Neuvillette used his true power to reseal the waters.

**Lyney, Lynette, Freminet & Arlecchino** — Three orphaned siblings
raised in the "House of the Hearth," an orphanage headed by the Fatui
Harbinger Arlecchino ("The Knave"). Born Peruere, Arlecchino grew up
under a predecessor "Mother" who ruled through violence, killed her to
free the children of the house, and was then pardoned by the Tsaritsa
herself and named the new Knave — one of the few Harbingers to clearly
show a humane side amid the Fatui machine.

**Clorinde & Chevreuse** — Two representatives of Fontaine's law
enforcement system: Clorinde is the undefeated Champion Duelist who
personally ended Callas exactly as he wished; Chevreuse commands the
Special Security and Surveillance Patrol, hard but not rigid.

## Character Profiles (Additional)

**Sigewinne** — Head nurse of the Fortress of Meropide, originally a
Melusine. She was once convicted and exiled to the very prison she now
serves for violating the ancient law forbidding "species-changing" — she
voluntarily changed her form to save a human friend. After being
pardoned, she chose to stay and care for the prisoners.

**Emilie** — A perfumer who investigates the mysterious revival of
"Auguste" — a legendary perfume line that went extinct after its
ingredient flower garden burned down 20 years ago. Leans toward personal
mystery rather than the main lore thread.

**Chiori** — An Inazuma-born fashion designer who left her homeland when
the Vision Hunt Decree and Sakoku Decree were issued, and rebuilt her
career in Fontaine.

## World Quests (Additional)

**The Origin of the Melusines** — Melusines are a new species that
appeared in Fontaine less than 400 years ago. According to the world
quest "Ancient Colors," the species originates from a battle that took
place inside the body of a colossal ancient creature named Elynas about
400 years ago — an explosion drove mechanical parts through Elynas's
body, and from those very wounds the Melusines were born. Elynas is
regarded as the "mother" of the entire Melusine race. It was Neuvillette
who brought the Melusine community out to live integrated with humans in
Fontaine's court — reinforcing his image as a figure standing on the
boundary between humanity and Fontaine's non-human entities.

**Ann of the Narzissenkreuz** — The Traveler meets a small oceanid named
Ann and helps rescue a Hydro mechanical bird from the evil dragon
Narcissus. Going back in history, the Narzissenkreuz Kingdom was once
conquered by Narcissus himself before he was defeated. The
"Narzissenkreuz Ordo" organization appearing in this story is confirmed
to be directly connected to the battle that gave birth to the Melusine
species (see the Melusine/Elynas origin section above).

**The Wild Fairy of Erinnyes** — A Melusine guarding the Erinnyes forest
is actually a vishap (an ancient sea dragon) in melusine form. The
Traveler helps her find three "keys" to purify the source of pollution
gnawing at the forest — a "guardian bearing a false identity" motif
similar to how many Archons/entities in the game must play a role to
protect what they love.

**Fontaine Research Institute Chronicles** — A major explosion at the
Fontaine Research Institute caused by an Arkhium energy core overloading
during an experiment. The Traveler helps investigate the wreckage,
revealing more about Fontaine's distinctive energy technology and the
dark side of an uncontrolled scientific research race — in the same
spirit as the "knowledge/technology beyond control" lesson repeated
across many other regions.

**Unfinished Comedy — "Game of the Rich"** — In the Fortress of Meropide,
a prisoner who calls himself a "monster" befriends a little girl born in
prison with no official identity outside, and tries to help her reunite
with her real family — portraying a rare humane side within Fontaine's
harsh prison system.
