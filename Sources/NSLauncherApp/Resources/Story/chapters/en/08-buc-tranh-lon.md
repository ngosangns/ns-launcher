# The Big Picture — the running mysteries

*The threads running through eight lands — tied together into a single
question.*

Across eight chapters, a few names keep recurring. After Nod-Krai and
Snezhnaya, most of them now have answers — not complete answers, but
enough to see the shape of the picture. The section below separates what
has been confirmed in the story from what remains speculation.

## Celestia & the Heavenly Principles

The power that actually runs Teyvat does not lie with the Archons, but
with the **Four Shades** — four Shades who are embodiments of the
Heavenly Principles: **Ronova** (death), **Istaroth** (time),
**Naberius**, and **Asmoday**, who is currently missing. They run Teyvat
like a system with regulations, and they themselves are bound by those
regulations: Ronova may only take two lives a day, and may not touch the
soul of a Descender.

The system's clearest instrument of restraint is the **Death Curse**:
whenever a technological breakthrough powerful enough to threaten
Celestia appears, everyone in the area dies temporarily and revives
remembering nothing. Not a punishment — just maintenance.

## Khaenri'ah

The godless underground kingdom, destroyed in the Cataclysm 500 years
ago. Two major pieces have been revealed: the people of Khaenri'ah are
**descendants of Anastasya Feodorovna's son** — the woman who later
became the Tsaritsa — so the four-pointed star pupils are a bloodline
inheritance, not a curse. And their king **Irmin** used an entire
apparatus to exterminate the descendants of the Crimson Moon dynasty,
creating at least one of the **Five Sinners** in the cruelest way
possible: forcing Rerir to kill his own fiancée.

## The Three Moons & kuuvahki

Teyvat once had three moons, created by **Nibelung** and then turned into
weapons in the war against the Heavenly Principles. Two moons shattered
and their goddesses died; only the Frost Moon survived, and it is because
of that that a fragile link to Teyvat remained — enough for Columbina to
be "born." The fragments that fell onto Nod-Krai made the land rich in
**kuuvahki**, a primordial energy predating even the seven elements.

The sky the people of Teyvat see is a false sky. This has now been
confirmed within the story itself, no longer community speculation.

## Irminsul → Aaru

Irminsul is the memory network that stores history and shapes Teyvat's
collective perception. It is **not a creation of the Heavenly
Principles** but one of many things they seized and repurposed from the
ancient dragons — according to Apep.

When Dottore infiltrated Irminsul through his own death, Nahida chose to
burn it down with the Pyro Gnosis, on reasoning the three Shades had to
accept: Irminsul exists to protect humanity, so when it becomes a danger
to humanity, it loses its reason to exist. **Aaru** — King Deshret's
soul-storage structure — now fulfills that role.

## Traveler, the Sibling & Paimon

The twins' spaceship sits on the **Frost Moon**, placed there by the
Heavenly Principles themselves. The flight log shows the ship flew past
Teyvat once thousands of years ago before coming back and crashing in
Nod-Krai. Why it was stored up there, and why it only flew past the first
time, remain unanswered.

Paimon, meanwhile, originates from **Celestia** — she was "pulled" down
to Khaenri'ah and offered to King Irmin as a gift he did not care about.
She has existed for at least 500 years, carries none of Celestia's power,
and has kept no fragment of memory from that time.

## Fatui & Tsaritsa

The goal has now been spoken aloud, no longer rumor: **to overthrow the
Heavenly Principles**. The Tsaritsa succeeded the Belyi Tsar during the
Cataclysm, bitter over what happened, severed ties with the other
Archons, and built the Fatui as the instrument to carry out that will
across Teyvat. She holds five of the six remaining Gnoses — the sole
exception being the **Pyro Gnosis**, which vanished without a trace after
Nahida used it to burn Irminsul.

But the Fatui are not a monolith. Capitano refused to steal Mavuika's
Gnosis and then offered up his own life. Columbina left the organization.
Arlecchino readily set political obligations aside for a friend. Sandrone
died to save a group of people who were not her subordinates. Dottore
betrayed them to crown himself a god and was dealt with by his own
allies. And Pantalone funded it all and never left his seat.

## Conclusion

The common thread across eight chapters: not a single Archon — Venti,
Zhongli, Raiden Ei, Nahida, Furina, Mavuika — is truly an obedient tool
of Celestia, and the strongest characters in the story are all defined by
what they accept losing rather than what they gain. Nahida burned her own
symbol. Mavuika gave away her Gnosis. Sandrone chose a death calculated
down to the last number. Columbina gave up the way home at the very
moment she had just found a name to call her own.

This is perhaps the shared emotional thread an original story inspired by
Teyvat could draw on: *even the divine must pay a price for being
themselves — and that price is usually set by their own hand.*
