# Prologue — Mondstadt

*Where the journey begins, and the god of wind walks disguised as a wandering bard.*

The Traveler and their sibling had just set foot in Teyvat when an unknown
god attacked them without warning: the sibling was taken captive, while the
Traveler fell to Mondstadt, losing most of their power and part of their
memory. In this land of freedom, the Traveler meets Paimon — a small
creature fished out of the sea — and the two become companions, setting out
on a journey to find the lost sibling while stumbling into the city's
biggest trouble.

## The Plot Around the Holy Lyre

The shadowy organization known as the Fatui is scheming to seize the Holy
Lyre der Himmel kept inside the cathedral, intending to summon and
manipulate Dvalin — the legendary wind dragon who was once Mondstadt's
loyal protector. The Traveler makes the acquaintance of Jean, Kaeya, Lisa,
and Venti — a cheerful, frequently drunk wandering bard who seems to know
far more than he lets on.

## Stormterror and the Gnawing Darkness

Dvalin suddenly goes berserk, attacking the city under the name
"Stormterror". The Traveler discovers that the dragon's mind is being
gnawed at by a dark force — later identified as the influence of the Abyss
Order, a faction that worships a "cursed queen" and is tied to the fallen
kingdom of Khaenri'ah. Diluc, a former knight turned aloof winery owner,
conducts his own parallel investigation and confronts the Fatui head-on.

> **Turning point.** Venti reveals himself to be Barbatos — the Anemo
> Archon, one of the Seven — living in disguise among the very people he
> protects. Diluc sets aside his differences and, together with Venti and
> the Traveler, journeys to Stormterror's lair, lifts the curse festering
> on Dvalin's back, and frees the wind dragon.

The Prologue closes by introducing two concepts that will run through the
entire game: *Visions* — items that grant elemental power to those chosen
by the Archons — and *Gnoses* — the powerful relics the Fatui are quietly
collecting from the Archons.

> **Open mysteries.** Where is the Traveler's sibling being held, and
> by whom? What are the Fatui collecting Gnoses for? What was Khaenri'ah,
> and why does it seem erased from collective memory? Why must the Archons
> hide themselves and limit their intervention in the world?

## Side Story: Dragonspine

Before it became a land of eternal frost, Dragonspine was the mountain
Vindagnyr — home to the ancient kingdom of Sal Vindagnyr, a civilization
once blessed by Celestia and built around a colossal Irminsul tree. Before
its people could complete the ritual to heal the tree through the
ley lines, one of the "celestial nails" Celestia drove into Teyvat
shattered into three fragments, causing the tree to wither and the whole
region to freeze forever — the sapling that grew afterward became the
Frostbearing Tree. Five hundred years ago, the Abyss dragon Durin was also
slain by Dvalin himself on this very peak; its blood seeped into the
ley lines, which is why the area teems with frost-corrupted creatures to
this day. Dragonspine thus stands as further proof that Celestia will
readily snuff out any civilization that outgrows its control — the same
pattern that would later repeat with Khaenri'ah.

## Character Profiles

**Kaeya** — Adopted by Crepus, Diluc's father, but the greatest secret of
his life is his Khaenri'ahn heritage — the godless kingdom that was
destroyed. He kept this identity hidden for years, confessing to Diluc
only right after Crepus passed away, which led to a fight and a rift
between the two brothers that has never fully healed. Though he carries
the bloodline of the Archons' age-old enemy, Kaeya still chose to serve
the Knights of Favonius to the end.

**Diluc** — His father Crepus died when a Delusion backfired on him while
fighting off monsters attacking a convoy. Right afterward, Kaeya confessed
his Khaenri'ahn origins, leaving Diluc to lose his father and discover in
the same blow that his closest brother bore the blood of the gods' enemy —
a double shock that drove him to leave the Knights of Favonius and hunt
down injustice on his own.

**Albedo** — Not a born human but a homunculus created by the alchemist
Rhinedottir ("Gold"), a native of Khaenri'ah. Rhinedottir is the one
behind many of the monsters that sowed terror across Teyvat during the
Cataclysm. Albedo has almost no memories from before he began following
her, and he is one of the few characters who realizes the Traveler does
not truly belong to Teyvat.

## Character Profiles (Additional)

**Jean** — She regards Vennessa — founder of the Knights of Favonius, who
overthrew the oppressive aristocracy of old and is now revered as a
goddess — as the ideal she models her life on.

**Mona** — Came to Mondstadt in poverty; notably, she knows the Traveler
does not belong to Teyvat and offers to read their fate. Her quest also
reveals that her old master once sent a diary to Alice — Klee's mother —
50 years ago.

**Eula** — A descendant of the Lawrence Clan, which once ruled Mondstadt
with an iron fist before the current order; her joining the Knights of
Favonius makes her family see her as a traitor. Her uncle later even
colluded with the Fatui in a plot to restore Mondstadt's "old order", only
to be stopped by Eula herself.

**Klee & Alice** — Alice is a renowned adventurer, founder of the
Hexenzirkel coven of witches, and Klee's mother; she is capable of
reshaping an entire archipelago (the Golden Apple Archipelago) as a gift
left for her daughter during a long absence.

**Rosaria** — Kidnapped by bandits as a child and trained as a killer, she
was taken in for rehabilitation at the Church of Favonius after the bandit
gang was wiped out. She calls herself a "child of the moonlight" rather
than one of the light — a contradiction between her religious facade and
her true nature.

**Fischl** — Her real name is Amy; the "Immernachtreich" and her title
"Prinzessin der Verurteilung" are a fantasy world she built for herself,
and Oz — her raven familiar — is a manifestation of her subconscious born
of her Vision, which she believes is real.

**Bennett** — An orphan of Natlan descent, raised in Mondstadt by a group
of retired adventurers, and plagued by bizarrely bad luck; a recent piece
of content reveals he hears a call from the land of Mare Jivari, hinting
at a fate bound to him since long ago.

**Lisa** — Once the most outstanding student the Sumeru Akademiya had seen
in two centuries, she grew disillusioned with her peers' reckless hunger
for knowledge and the fear of its price, so she abandoned the scholar's
path to become a librarian in Mondstadt.

*(Sucrose, Noelle, Amber, Mika, Barbara: their story quests mainly sketch
personality, with no major lore reveals tied to the main storyline.)*

**Venti** — His own Story Quest, the "Carmen Dei Chapter": the Traveler
and Paimon meet a little girl playing with an "imaginary friend",
reminding the Traveler that their sibling also had a similar imaginary
friend in childhood. Venti carries a special device to track traces of an
old friend long gone — portraying the lonely, nostalgic side of an Archon
who has lived through centuries, in contrast to the carefree front he
shows in the main Archon Quest.

## World Quests (Additional)

No world quest chain in Mondstadt carries lore on the scale of
Dragonspine/The Chasm — most world quests here are small-scale affairs of
exploring locations or minor side characters, not directly tied to the
Celestia/Khaenri'ah/Fatui mystery arc.

**The Sword Graveyard (Dadaupa Gorge)** — Rows of swords planted in the
ground mark the graves of warriors who fell in the battle against the
monsters from Khaenri'ah — rare physical evidence that Mondstadt was a
direct battlefield of the Cataclysm 500 years ago. The quest also touches
an older layer of history: before Barbatos's time, Mondstadt was harshly
ruled by a previous Anemo Archon (Decarabian), and the ancient wolf
Andrius only submitted after seeing Barbatos's power far surpassed his
own.

**A Yaksha's Wish (Jueyun Karst)** — A ruined stone tablet bears a message
from Bosacius, an old comrade of Xiao: after Liyue found peace, nearly all
the other yakshas had fallen, leaving only Xiao and Bosacius; Bosacius
expresses his wish to depart this world and rest — a small piece that
directly supplements the yakshas' burden of "karmic debt".
