# Chapter III — Sumeru

*A god imprisoned by her own academy, and knowledge made forbidden.*

Sumeru is a land of rainforest and the Akademiya, an academy of knowledge.
The Traveler meets Tighnari and his pupil Collei, who suffers from a
strange disease that hardens her body into scales. Sumeru once had two
Dendro gods: Rukkhadevata — Greater Lord Rukkhadevata, who "vanished"
several hundred years ago — and Kusanali — the current Lesser Lord
Kusanali, almost entirely absent from public life even though scholars
still worship her deceased predecessor.

## The God Imprisoned in Her Own Mind

The Traveler discovers that Kusanali is actually Nahida — a little girl
imprisoned in a private space of consciousness, completely cut off from
the real world, by the Akademiya's own leadership. The reason: Irminsul —
the system storing the memories of all Teyvat — is being contaminated by
"forbidden knowledge", causing a withering of life that is the root cause
of Collei's disease. Nahida, though imprisoned, still secretly helps her
people through dreams — the very thing that got her isolated.

Together with Cyno and the skeptical scholar Alhaitham, the Traveler
uncovers the truth: Rukkhadevata did not vanish naturally but sacrificed
herself 500 years ago — right after the catastrophe that struck the
kingdom of Khaenri'ah — to save Irminsul from collapse. The Akademiya
deliberately concealed her death to preserve the scholars' order of power.

## The Discarded Puppet

A Fatui Harbinger breaks in to kidnap Nahida — Scaramouche, who calls
himself a "defective product" cast aside by the Fatui. His backstory
gradually comes to light: he was once a puppet created by Raiden Ei to
rule Inazuma in her stead, then abandoned by his own "mother", after which
he wandered into the Fatui, bearing a grudge against the Archons for
believing he had been discarded.

> **Turning point.** Instead of destroying Scaramouche, Nahida chooses to
> heal his mind, awakening the humanity buried within him. He lets go of
> his old identity and is reborn as the Wanderer — the first Harbinger to
> openly turn his back on the Fatui. Nahida is recognized as the true
> Dendro Archon and opens Irminsul's store of knowledge to all people
> instead of leaving it the scholars' monopoly.

While searching Irminsul, Nahida discovers that the Traveler's sibling
once appeared in Khaenri'ah before Celestia brought down the catastrophe
that destroyed the kingdom for the crime of "possessing forbidden
knowledge" — yet the records around that period are abnormally vague, as
if someone deliberately erased them.

> **Open mysteries.** What exactly was the "forbidden knowledge" that got
> Khaenri'ah punished? What was the sibling's true role in Khaenri'ah
> before the catastrophe? What does Pierro — the first Harbinger, once a
> royal mage of Khaenri'ah — know that he has yet to reveal?

## Side Story: The Desert and the Aranyaka

The Aranyaka world quest chain, told through the words of the Aranara
(little forest creatures), reveals a desert civilization even older than
Rukkhadevata's time, revolving around the "three eternal companions":
Rukkhadevata, the Goddess of Flowers Nabu Malikata, and King Deshret.
Deshret once dreamed of a world unbound by the Heavenly Principles — a
utopia built on science and human will. That dream collapsed when a form
of forbidden knowledge spread and caused corruption, forcing Nabu
Malikata to sacrifice herself to stop it. Deshret's tragedy thus serves as
a "historical draft" for the pattern of punishment Khaenri'ah would later
suffer: anyone who seeks knowledge or self-determination beyond the
bounds Celestia permits must pay the price.

## Character Profiles

**Alhaitham** — A cold, rational scholar of the Haravatat Darshan, serving
as the Akademiya's Scribe while constantly questioning the very system he
works for. His quarrelsome roommate relationship with the architect Kaveh
is the emotional core, embodying two extremes: one who believes in serving
ideals to the point of exhaustion, one who keeps a rational distance from
every institution.

**Cyno** — The General Mahamatra leading the Matra, the terror of scholars
who abuse forbidden knowledge. His every action is recorded and analyzed
by the Akasha system (the Akademiya's knowledge/prediction network), yet
over time he has grown harder for that very system to predict.

**Wanderer** — After being redeemed by Nahida, Scaramouche returns under a
new identity but initially lives adrift. Nahida helps him realize that
much of the betrayal he blamed on the Archons was in fact staged by the
Fatui Harbinger Il Dottore. After making peace with his past, he stays in
Sumeru as a student and unofficial assistant to Nahida.

**Dehya** — Tied to the nomadic Eremite communities of the desert and the
memory of King Deshret's ancient kingdom. Her birth father once led the
largest Eremite organization, maintaining power by holding blackmail
dossiers on his own comrades — a heavy legacy Dehya must face after his
death.

## Character Profiles (Additional)

**Collei** — Before coming to Mondstadt and meeting Amber, Collei was a
victim of the Fatui: afflicted with Eleazar since childhood, she was taken
into human experimentation by an organization posing as a charity set up
by the Fatui, which implanted dark flame power into her body and caused
her to unwittingly harm others — one of the clearest proofs of the Fatui's
ruthless side: willing to use sick children as test subjects.

**Kaveh** — The architect who designed the Palace of Alcazarzaray, a work
destroyed by the Withering just as it neared completion, forcing him into
debt to rebuild it and into living with Alhaitham. Behind his idealistic
exterior, he carries guilt over his father's death and the hardship of
poverty.

**Nilou, Candace, Faruzan, Layla, Tighnari** — Mostly character and
cultural sketches of Sumeru (dance arts, descendants of King Deshret,
astrology, rainforest ecology), revealing no major new lore for the main
storyline.

## World Quests (Additional)

**King Deshret and Apep** — Deshret was one of the "three eternal
companions" who governed ancient Sumeru, ruling the desert with ancient
technology. Before dying in the Archon War, he made a pact with Apep — the
ancient Dendro Dragon: in exchange for Apep staying out of the desert, he
allowed the dragon to "devour" him after death to inherit his knowledge —
including the "forbidden knowledge" that gradually warped Apep into a
dangerous ancient dragon. This is another link in the motif repeated
throughout the game: knowledge beyond permitted limits always leaves a
corroding aftermath, the same pattern as Khaenri'ah and Nabu Malikata's
tragedy.

**Varuna Gatha (Vanarana)** — A continuation of the Aranyaka chain: in the
Vanarana area of Dharma Forest, the climate turns abnormal because the
"Varuna machine" has gone unmaintained since its creators died long ago —
expanding on the ancient civilization that once used machinery to sustain
the forest's ecological balance, the same recurring theme of "abandoned
ancient technology" seen throughout Sumeru's lore.

**Vimana Agama (Devantaka Mountain)** — Devantaka Mountain ("destroyer of
gods") is home to a colossal Ruin Golem once studied by a legendary
scholar, leading to a catastrophic accident. The Traveler eventually gets
inside this giant machine and must activate it — further proof that
ancient Sumeru possessed mechanical technology far beyond the ordinary, in
the same vein as the Ruin Machines/Guards scattered across desert and
rainforest.

**Golden Slumber (Great Red Sand)** — A group of researchers excavating
the ruins of Khaj-Nisut accidentally awakens an ancient prayer capable of
releasing an entity named Jeht. When one of them recites the prayer, he
falls into the "Golden Slumber" — a collective dream realm where every
individual consciousness dissolves into one, nearly impossible to return
from. His friend is almost persuaded to stay and merge into that dream
before being pulled awake just in time — adding detail about the
civilization of King Deshret's era and the dangerous nature of the relics
left in the desert.

**Memory's Final Chapter** — The true ending of the Aranyaka chain: the
Traveler helps three Aranara reunite with an elderly researcher who longs
to meet them, though his memories of the Aranara keep fading with time —
an emotional farewell that officially closes the entire Aranyaka arc.
