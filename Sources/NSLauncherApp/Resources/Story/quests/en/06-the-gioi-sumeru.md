# World Quests — Sumeru

*The rainforest of the Aranara and the desert of King Deshret: two
halves of a land that lives on memory, and the price paid to keep that
memory.*

Sumeru has the largest volume of World Quests in the game to date. Its
world-quest system splits cleanly into two halves: the northern
rainforest revolving around the Aranara, the Ashvattha trees and the
Withering (Marana) phenomenon; and the Great Red Sand together with the
Desert of Hadramaveth revolving around the legacy of King Deshret, the
Eremite tribes and the Jinni. There is also the Khvarena branch in the
Gavireh Lajavard region — where the lore of Khaenri'ah and the
Cataclysm shows through most clearly in all of Sumeru.

This document lists every chain and sub-quest name in the order the
wiki records them, with plot summaries for the major chains.

---

## Aranyaka — "Book of the Forest"

Sumeru's longest World Quest chain, in four Parts. The Traveler meets
Rana, a Forest Watcher of Vimara Village, and is drawn into the secret
of the Aranara — plant creatures visible only to those who still keep
their innocence. When Rana is infected by "Marana" (the Withering)
after rushing in to save a child, the only way to save her is to grow a
Bija fruit, and that demands an entire Aranara festival plus the
destruction of Marana's avatar hidden beneath Old Vanarana. The final
price is steep: Arama sacrifices himself to become a new Ashvattha
tree, while Arana trades away all of his memories to bear fruit. The
chain closes with Rana waking up, receiving a Dendro Vision, and
inviting the very friend who has forgotten her to set out on a journey.

A notable detail: beneath Old Vanarana lies an old battlefield of the
Cataclysm 500 years ago — full of Ruin Machines and rifthounds —
showing the Aranara once fought the invasion on this very ground.

## Varuna Gatha

The Aranara Arapandu asks the Traveler to repair the Varuna Contraption
— the weather-regulating machine in Apam Woods that broke down after
its creators passed away and "Nara Varuna" disappeared. After
re-tuning the four machine clusters with the other Aranara, the group
enters the core and defeats a Winged Dendroshroom sabotaging the
system. Completing this chain unlocks the ability to change the weather
in Apam Woods with the lyre.

## Agnihotra Sutra

The Traveler helps Aranakin, Aragaru and Aranaga gather three
ingredients for the Arahaoma ritual — the thing that opens the way into
Mawtiyima to heal the forest. After making the offering to the
Jamikayomars and receiving approval, the group goes inside and sweeps
the Marana clean. A short chain, but one of the four mandatory branches
of Part II.

## An Unwavering Culinary Dream

Arapacati asks the Traveler to find four Aranara "brothers" wandering
the forest learning to cook. It turns out their passion for cooking
stems from "Nara Varuna" — the Traveler's lost sibling — who taught
the Aranara how humans eat. The chain ends with preparing a dish for
humans at the festival.

## Vimana Agama

The Aranara ask the Traveler to investigate the contamination around
Devantaka Mountain, leading to Jazari — a Kshahrewar researcher
studying a Khaenri'ah Ruin Golem. The contamination source is the Chaos
Core, removed and sealed beneath Dev's Cavern, now leaking. To open the
way, the group must restore the Amiti device from three parts, pulling
in the story of three childhood friends in Vimara Village — the third
friend, forgotten by the other two, being the Aranara Ararycan. Inside
the Ruin Golem, the Traveler clashes with Ynghildr, a Khaenri'ah knight
twisted by the curse into an Abyss creature; beneath Dev's Cavern, the
Abyss Order turns out to be behind the contamination.

## Golden Slumber

The opening chain of the entire desert storyline. The Traveler takes a
job finding a missing archaeology team and meets the core cast: the
scholar Tirzad, the merchant Nachtigal, and the Eremite mercenary
father-daughter pair Jebrael and Jeht. The Scarlet Sand Slate and the
little machine Benben gradually unlock King Deshret's ruins, from
Khemenu Temple to the Mausoleum of King Deshret and then Khaj-Nisut.
Jebrael's past comes out: Jeht's mother was murdered by Samail — the
Eremite leader of the Thutmose tribe — to keep a hold on him. At the
throne of Khaj-Nisut, Samail steps into the "Golden Slumber", an
eternal dream realm from which there is no return; Jebrael is forced to
sacrifice himself into it to seize control of the structure and open
the way for the group to escape.

## The Dirge of Bilqis

The continuation of *Golden Slumber*. Jeht invites the Traveler to the
Tanit Camps to fulfill her wish of burying her parents at the Eternal
Oasis (The Orchard of Pairidaeza). The way in only opens with a Jinni
fragment, leading to the awakening of Liloupar — the Jinni lady sealed
in a bottle, who signs a pact of service with the Traveler. The journey
through King Deshret's old canal system exposes two traitors in the
Tanit tribe, Aderfi and Azariq, along with the Fatui hand behind them.
At the chain's end, the group reaches the oasis — a garden frozen in
time — and Liloupar realizes that Nabu Malikata, the Goddess of
Flowers, has been dead for ages: King Deshret lied to her and the Jinni
for centuries.

## Apocalypse Lost

A chain of exactly one quest, unlocked after placing all five chess
pieces on the Chessboard of Safhe Shatranj (four obtained during *The
Dirge of Bilqis*, the last from *"The Falcon's Hunt"*). This is
Liloupar's retelling of her past: she was once the Jinni queen of the
city of Gurabad, married the human leader Ormazd, then realized he was
a tyrant. Her revenge — including unleashing a black plague borrowed
from the Abyss's power — destroyed the entire city and turned her own
son Parvezravan into a monster. At the chain's end, Liloupar dissolves
her pact with the Traveler to head toward her own "dark destiny".

## Her Foes Rage Like Great Waters...

The finale of the Jeht storyline. A series of staged events makes the
Traveler and Jeht each believe the other has betrayed them: the
Traveler is ambushed by assassins "sent by Jeht", while Jeht is told
the Traveler has joined hands with the Fatui. Investigating the Fatui
camps reveals Jeht was actually kidnapped. The puppeteer is Babel, the
Tanit matriarch — she wants the Traveler dead so she can become
Liloupar's new master and use the Eternal Oasis as a springboard to
overthrow Sumeru. Jeht chooses friendship over tribe, finishes Babel
with her own hands, then departs with Benben as someone who belongs
nowhere — which, in the Traveler's reading, is freedom, not loss.

*(The chain name and quest names all draw on Bible passages about the
fall of Babylon — the same root as the name Babel.)*

## Old Notes and New Friends

A branch parallel to *The Dirge of Bilqis*, also unlocked after *Golden
Slumber*. The two researchers Kalantari and Soheil in Aaru Village
initially dismiss every one of the Traveler's findings for lack of
"academic authority". Soheil assigns the Traveler to verify the
hypotheses in his old notes, certain nothing will turn up. The result
is the opposite: everything in the notes checks out, the Traveler
climbs the access-authority tiers of the Mausoleum of King Deshret's
mechanisms, and Soheil is forced to re-examine the Akademiya's rigid
framework. Completing *Dual Evidence* permanently shuts off the
sandstorm vortex atop the mausoleum.

## Khvarena of Good and Evil

The major chain in the Gavireh Lajavard region, where the phenomenon
called "The Sign of Apaosha" is gnawing at the land. The Pari Sorush
names the Traveler her Yasnapati, and together they collect the Great
Songs of Khvarena to perform the Rite of Chinvat. Nasejuna, leader of
the Order of Skeptics, accompanies them for most of the journey before
showing his hand: he only pretended to revere the Pari in order to
seize the Great Songs and break the Abyss seal, believing the history
of Khaenri'ah more trustworthy than his own sect's legends. The ending
opens one of Sumeru's most important lore threads: Zurvan recounts the
events of 500 years ago, when she fought the Abyss alongside a masked
swordsman (Dainsleif) and a one-armed sage (Nagarjuna) — and that
swordsman left together with a golden-haired person.

## A Gifted Rose

The continuation of *The Exile*. The old man Affan living alone at the
village's edge turns out to be the gardener Sabbah mentioned; the
Traveler in turn clears the nursery, waters the plants, then "awakens"
the seeds the way Sabbah taught, until the Golden Rose blooms before
everyone's eyes. The chain has a follow-up Hidden Exploration

## The Gourmet Supremos (Sumeru portion)

A cross-region chain that begins in Inazuma and has two quests set in
Sumeru. The Gourmet Supremos come to Sumeru so Parvaneh can submit her
research results to the Akademiya, then get tangled in a dispute
between the tutor Farzah and the merchant Nète. The Traveler gathers
ingredients all over Sumeru so Farzah's research succeeds; Nète
concedes and offers a partnership, while the group prepares to depart
for Fontaine.

## Scope Notes

- **Sacred Mountain** and **Fragments of Ancient Songs** are not Sumeru
  World Quest chains. The wiki has no page under exactly those names
  belonging to Sumeru; "Ancient Sacred Mountain" is a sub-area of
  Natlan, and near matches for "Fragments of Ancient Songs" are all
  items/instruments rather than quests. If needed, these two entries
  belong in the Natlan document's scope.
- **Dirge of Bilqis** and **The Dirge of Bilqis** are the same chain;
  the wiki records only a single page, with the article "The".
- Sumeru's Archon Quests (Chapter III) are not covered in this
  document.
- Some chains (*Pale Fire*, *Monumental Study*, *The Exile*, *A Gifted
  Rose*) currently have no plot summary on the wiki; the descriptions
  here are built from each sub-quest's intro line.
